My research aims to explain biological processes and how they change, by bringing together molecular biology, cell biology and computational biology. My training spans biochemistry, biophysics and computer science. I combine experimental assays with mathematical modelling, machine learning and image analysis to narrow down complex biological questions and generate testable hypotheses.

Research domains
======

### Geroscience and neurological disorders

I am interested in how epigenetic information and chromatin architecture change across the lifespan, at the intersection of geroscience, neuroepigenetics and 3D genome biology. DNA methylation clocks describe biological age. I want to understand the chromatin mechanisms beneath them, and how DNA methylation, chromatin accessibility and genome organization shape cellular identity in the developing and aging brain. This domain also covers how disrupted epigenetic states relate to neurodevelopmental disorders and age-related neurological dysfunction.

### Spatial and multi-omics technology development

Molecular genetics is moving toward technologies that capture biological systems at higher resolution, across multiple modalities and in clinically relevant material. My interests here include sequencing and imaging-based chromatin profiling, molecular profiling of both fresh and archival (FFPE) tissues, and AI/ML frameworks to integrate the resulting datasets. I apply these tools to understand how therapeutic agents, especially epigenetic drugs, reshape chromatin and cellular states, with relevance to precision medicine and drug repurposing.

### Epigenetic inheritance

I am also interested in how epigenetic information links aging, disease susceptibility and inheritance in mammals. This includes how DNA methylation interacts with chromatin and regulatory RNAs, and how epigenetic states persist or are reset across generations. Studying this requires an integrated multi-omics view of DNA methylation, histone organization, chromatin architecture and RNA-mediated regulation.

Research so far
======

### Doctoral research

During my Ph.D. at Jadavpur University, I studied intrinsically disordered proteins in neurodegenerative diseases and cancer. This included SOD1 in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and alpha-synuclein in Parkinson's disease. I used evolutionary sequence–structure analysis, graph-theoretical and mathematical models, and unsupervised learning to relate structural malleability to function. I later extended this work to transcription factors as potential drug targets, using protein disorder and moonlighting to explain their functional versatility.

### Postdoctoral and current research

In 2021 I joined Dr. Sriharsa Pradhan's lab at New England Biolabs. There I helped develop NEED-seq, a sequencing assay for profiling chromatin-associated proteins, and built computational pipelines for chromatin accessibility, DNA methylation and hydroxymethylation, and 3D genome data. My work focuses on the interplay between epigenetic writers, readers and erasers, histone modifications and cell-fate decisions. It extends to DNA methylation dynamics during differentiation, chromatin responses to epigenetic drugs, deep-learning analysis of chromatin imaging, and chromatin profiling of archival tissue. I also collaborate with Prof. Melanie Ehrlich (Tulane University) and Prof. Petra Hajkova (Imperial College London).
